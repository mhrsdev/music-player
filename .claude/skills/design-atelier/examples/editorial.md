# Worked example: a coastal architecture journal

**Brief:** Create a small, original editorial front page for readers interested in how coastal buildings respond to weather. No photography or brand assets are supplied. Provide working article discovery and navigation, without implying a real publication or commissioned project.

**Contract:** A reader should identify the subject, inspect the lead story and reach its reading view. The local demonstration uses fictional editorial content and original vector linework. It does not provide a CMS, subscription service or researched architectural findings.

**Direction:** A clear blue field and a large white architectural section drawing make the subject recognizable before secondary copy. Compact sans-serif navigation supports an expressive serif headline. The drawing provides an alternative to generic stock imagery while remaining lightweight and self-contained. This is a particular concept, not a style prescription for all journals.

**Simpler alternatives:** A text-only index would be sufficient functionally, but communicates the subject less directly. A WebGL building would add implementation and access costs without helping this reading task. Choose the lightweight vector composition.

**Structure:** masthead → lead subject and drawing → readable article excerpt → field-note index. The primary action navigates to actual content. Secondary article disclosures are native controls rather than fake links.

**States and adaptation:** narrow screens stack title and drawing while retaining the reading order; native disclosure elements expose additional notes; focus remains visible; missing scripts do not hide content; reduced motion does not remove meaning. The illustration is decorative support for visible copy, not the sole source of important information.

**Critique target:** check whether the title competes with the drawing at small sizes, whether the story excerpt is readable and whether content anchors reach the intended section. The initial vector draft is an illustration, not a photograph or evidence of a built project.

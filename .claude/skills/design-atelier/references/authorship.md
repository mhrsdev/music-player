# Authorship: does it look generated?

A page can pass every usability check and still read as made from the average of its category. Readers sense that within seconds, and it costs trust: the work stops belonging to its subject. The sameness is older than generative tools (template kits and Bootstrap produced it for years), and a 2026 count of 1,590 Show HN sites found about a fifth with four or more scripted patterns, so the question is about authorship, not about accusing a tool. Take this pass after the three perspectives in [self-critique](self-critique.md), looking at the render as an editor who has seen a thousand generated pages. [The catalog](authorship-catalog.md) lists the tells with a repair move for each.

## Why it happens

When a brief leaves gaps, a model fills them with the most probable choice, and prompts made of adjectives such as clean, modern or bold pull toward the average, since, one analysis argues, those are the commonest words in design writing. A 2026 conceptual paper on vibe coding names three moments where it gets through: a vague intent, a clean instant render that feels finished, and refinement rounds that accept what came back. Removing one default without making a decision moves the page to the next most probable look, which is why warm cream with a serif and a terracotta accent, near-black with one neon accent, and newspaper hairlines with tracked all-caps labels now mark a second wave. Treat this pass as deliberate friction against all of it. The repair is never "take it out"; it is "decide this from the subject", and when no decision comes, cut the element, because ornament with no job is the real fault. A sterile result usually means the direction was missing, so return to the thesis ([judgment](judgment.md)).

## How to look

1. Say in a sentence what this page leads with, then list eight or so things that belong only to this subject: its materials, units, documents, vocabulary, people, constraints. Every repair draws from that list.
2. Locate before you judge. Read the signals from `scripts/inspect_page.py` as a starting point, then look at the screenshots yourself, first screen first. Several tells together on the first screen outweigh many scattered below it, and one alone is rarely a finding.
3. Run the competitor test. Could the nearest competitor, or any product in the category, use this headline, structure or image unchanged? If so, it is a default.
4. Run the claim test on structure. Each section should carry one claim and its proof. A section that exists because pages have one (a logo row, a testimonial, an FAQ, a closing banner) and holds no claim of yours should go.
5. Repair by decision. Replace a habit with the subject's own artifact, let the structure follow the content's shape, or cut. Add one specific element that only this product could have, such as a real screen or an honestly labeled sample from its own world. Two or three considered moves beat a sweep.
6. Check the replacement against the second-wave looks, re-render, and confirm nothing functional, accessible or truthful was lost.

## Families of tells

Each is a reason to look closer. The [catalog](authorship-catalog.md) names the individual tells and repairs.

- **First screen:** gradient or glow hero, centered stack with a pill badge, stock palette, stock typeface.
- **Type and surface:** one neutral sans for everything, a lone styled word, labels above every heading, unearned gradients, glass, stacked border-shadow-tint.
- **Structure:** the stock section order, identical card grids, sections of equal weight, everything centered.
- **Components and motion:** tinted icon tiles, side stripes, arrows on buttons, the same entrance and hover on every block.
- **Copy:** filler and promotional words, contrast slogans, round invented numbers, stock names, interchangeable sentences.
- **Imagery:** blobs, unfinished hand-coded scenes, fake dashboards with invented figures.
- **Completeness:** links to nowhere, missing states, hover-only actions, placeholders left behind.
- **Right-to-left pages:** layouts that are left-to-right with translated words.

## Guardrails

No single tell proves anything. People and template kits make the same choices, untrained readers judged machine-made text near chance in some studies, and an analyst's own scripted list of sixteen patterns still produced roughly five to ten percent false positives on manual checks. Weigh clusters and locations, not counts alone. The user's or project's stated direction always wins: if they asked for cream, a serif and terracotta, deliver it and make it excellent. Keep what works, and do not strip meaning, state, access or required content to look less generated; a label that holds information its heading lacks can stay, as can a numbered list that is a real sequence, a card grid of truly parallel items and a dark theme that suits the product. Do not fill removed ornament with other ornament, and do not add quirks such as noise, tilt, doodles or custom cursors; they are not authorship. Do not accept your own claim that a design is unique as evidence; ask for the locatable details that make it so. Vocabulary tells change with each model generation, so use the principle that outlasts any word list: vague, promotional, interchangeable language with no particulars.

## Reporting

Say what you found with its location, what you changed and the decision that replaced it, and what you kept on purpose and why. Do not give a slop score; the evidence is the render. The signals from `scripts/inspect_page.py` are counted by simple rules and can be wrong either way, and a page with none is not thereby well made.

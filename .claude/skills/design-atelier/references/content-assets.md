# Content, imagery and icons

Use realistic content before fine layout. A product feels generic when its words could fit any business, even with carefully chosen colors. Describe what the product does and what happens next in the user's vocabulary. Keep action labels stable: “Save changes” should produce “Changes saved,” not an unrelated “Operation completed.”

## Content hierarchy

Write for the question at that point in the journey. An introduction explains relevance; evidence supports a claim; instructions help action. Avoid decorative labels, arbitrary numbered sections and unsupported superlatives. Labels should communicate structure rather than fill empty space. A sequence can be numbered; an unordered set of benefits usually does not need to be.

Use honest, specific proof. Never fabricate customer logos, testimonials, conversion gains, security certifications, availability or case-study outcomes. Clearly identify prototype records as demonstration data. A mock state must not imply that a real reservation, payment or message was completed.

## UX writing

Use concrete verbs and concise sentences. Match tone to the situation: a failure needs clarity and recovery, not a playful joke. Explain what happened, what remains saved and how to proceed. Write empty states according to their cause: first use, no filter matches, permissions or missing connection need different remedies.

Preserve essential details such as price, units, time zone, eligibility and consequences before commitment. Do not hide unfavorable facts in low-contrast text or use visual emphasis to manipulate a decision.

## Imagery

Choose images that reveal the subject or provide evidence. Establish a coherent crop, lighting, scale and treatment. Avoid a generic decorative image where the product itself would explain more. Use supplied licensed assets when available. Track source and rights for reused work; do not infer a license from a downloadable URL.

Provide meaningful alt text for informative images and empty alt text for purely decorative images. Do not duplicate a neighboring caption mechanically. Use dimensions and responsive sources to prevent shifts and excessive downloads. Important text should remain text rather than being baked into a picture.

## Icons

Prefer an existing coherent icon set. Keep stroke, optical weight, alignment and metaphors consistent. Pair unfamiliar icons with visible labels. An icon-only button needs an accessible name; a tooltip alone is unreliable for touch. Directional icons can mirror with locale; logos, play symbols, photographs and every diagram should not be mirrored indiscriminately.

Inspect real long names, long translations, missing media and user-generated strings. Content resilience is part of the design, not a later cleanup task.

# Composition, hierarchy and optical craft

Start from content priority and a reading path. Establish one dominant relationship, then give supporting elements an appropriate emphasis. Size, contrast, position, isolation and detail all affect prominence; they should not all compete simultaneously.

## Group before decorating

Use proximity and alignment to show what belongs together. The gap inside a group should usually be visibly smaller than the gap between groups. Apply borders, backgrounds and cards when they communicate a boundary, state or object. If every paragraph becomes a card, the containment loses meaning.

Define content rails and a small spacing vocabulary. Choose dimensions from actual content and viewing conditions, not a sacred grid number. A consistent rhythm supports intentional exceptions: a major work sample may deserve extra space while a label and value remain tightly paired. Avoid converting every local measurement into a globally reusable token.

## Balance visual mass

Evaluate large areas of dark, saturated or detailed content. A small high-contrast shape can outweigh a much larger pale region. Balance does not require symmetry; asymmetry needs a stable relationship and a comprehensible reading order. Do not use CSS reordering to create a visual path that contradicts keyboard and screen reader order.

Keep actionable controls close to the content they affect. A sticky summary can preserve orientation in a long workflow, but it should not obscure fields or consume the entire short viewport. A spacious public page and a productive workbench can share quality without sharing density.

## Pacing

Across a long page, vary scale and content form in response to the story: overview, evidence, detail, decision. Repetition can improve comparison for products or records; monotonous repeated sections weaken a narrative. Do not insert giant headings or alternating backgrounds simply to create artificial variety.

Treat the image crop as part of composition. Preserve the subject, direction of attention and important detail. On small screens, use an alternative crop or a changed composition when shrinking loses meaning. Decorative text embedded in an image is a poor substitute for adaptable, accessible text.

## Optical refinement

Inspect actual type and icons at normal size. Align perceived centers, baseline and edge weight, allowing small corrections where strict boxes look wrong. Keep controls' hit areas stable and sufficiently large. Use optical corrections sparingly and explain unusual adjustments in code when necessary.

Verify the whole frame, a representative detail and an awkward content state. Check long titles, sparse and crowded lists, missing images, text zoom and narrow containers. Fix hierarchy and spacing relationships before fine shadow tuning. Do not infer quality from source code alone.

# Discovery and evidence

Read the requested deliverable first. Extract the main person, situation, task, current obstacle and desired outcome. A product category is context, not a design brief: a hospital procurement dashboard and a patient appointment page demand different decisions.

## Inspect available truth

Read relevant project instructions, routes, component and token files, package versions, real content and existing screenshots. Inspect a representative working screen before proposing a redesign. Identify valuable behavior and identity to preserve. Distinguish explicit requirements from defaults inherited accidentally from a starter template.

Organize observations as supplied, directly observed, inferred or unknown. An inferred persona is a working hypothesis, not user research. Competitor pages can reveal conventions but cannot establish what this product's users need. Do not claim interviews, tests, market leadership or conversion improvements without evidence.

Write the core job concretely: “An on-call operator identifies a stalled shipment and contacts its owner.” Identify frequency, urgency, cost of error, information required and the decision that completes the task. Use these to choose density, hierarchy and safeguards. A marketing site may prioritize understanding and qualified contact; a daily work surface prioritizes scan speed and continuity.

## Resolve uncertainty proportionately

Ask when the missing answer changes the product, platform, brand constraint or critical journey. Do not ask the user to restate information already in the repository or conversation. For reversible choices, state a compact assumption and move. If a consequential dependency is unresolved, continue work that does not depend on it rather than inventing an answer.

On an open creative brief, propose a concrete subject-specific direction. Keep public claims provisional until supplied. Synthetic content may support a prototype when clearly identified as demonstration data; avoid fabricated social proof, medical claims, financial promises or customer identities.

## Choose a learning method

- To learn whether the structure is comprehensible, propose a task-based usability session using realistic tasks and observe completion, errors and questions.
- To compare appearance, use matched content and states, conceal variant labels if possible, and ask evaluators about fit as well as preference.
- To inspect technical behavior, run reproducible checks; they answer a narrower question than human usability testing.

For small changes, this reasoning can be a few sentences. For a new application, keep a brief with the critical journeys, constraints, unknowns and acceptance conditions. Revisit it when new evidence changes the premise. Do not fill a large template simply to simulate thoroughness.

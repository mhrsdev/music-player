# Product patterns without style stereotypes

Use these as obligations to investigate, not page templates to reproduce. Combine with [information architecture](information-architecture.md) and [interaction](interaction.md).

## Public and editorial sites

Make subject, relevance and next action understandable. Choose a narrative shaped by the material: a journal may lead with an actual story, a museum with an artifact, a product with a useful demonstration. Use truthful proof and functional contact or navigation. Long-form reading needs a good measure, clear heading hierarchy and understandable metadata. Decorative labels and invented reading statistics do not improve credibility.

## Commerce

Support discovery, product comparison, stock and variant decisions, full price understanding, cart recovery and checkout errors. Show delivery, recurring commitments and consequential terms before commitment. A prototype may demonstrate these states, but must not simulate a completed payment as a real transaction. Use an established provider's production payment flow when implementation scope includes payment; never collect card data casually in a custom mock form.

## Booking and scheduling

Expose availability, duration, location or channel, relevant price and time zone. Preserve selections when going back. Handle a slot becoming unavailable, conflicting choices, validation and a clear confirmation. Distinguish request submitted from reservation confirmed. Calendar conventions and local dates require product-specific decisions; the user's language alone is insufficient.

## SaaS and administration

Prioritize activation, durable work, scope and permissions. Orient users within the correct account, tenant or workspace. Make autosave, unsaved work and connection states explicit. Settings need clear consequence and feedback; destructive actions need proportionate safeguards. Do not hide a complex product behind a decorative marketing-like dashboard.

## Learning and documentation

Make progress, resumption, search, versions and content structure understandable. Preserve learner work. Provide captions or transcripts when relevant. Documentation needs stable links, navigable sections and readable code; a successful search should not merely return a visually attractive but unhelpful card.

## Health, finance and public services

Favor clarity, eligibility, consequences, privacy and recoverability. Avoid unearned trust badges, unsupported clinical claims and false urgency. Use straightforward language and accessible error recovery. Do not require excessive personal data just to make a screen look complete. Follow actual legal/product requirements rather than inventing compliance claims.

## Adaptation

For maps, media, games and spatial tools, retain domain-specific controls and accessible equivalents where possible. Use the smallest relevant specialist reference; do not activate every product pattern. Verify the actual primary journey and a meaningful exception. User research and production behavior remain separate from a plausible design hypothesis.
